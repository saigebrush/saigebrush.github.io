Master's Thesis

This project aims to establish the first methodological framework for applying Electron Spin Resonance (ESR) dating to glacial deposits in North America. The study focuses on late Pleistocene glacial deposits in the Tahoe and Donner basins, where independent age control from cosmogenic 10Be exposure dating and infrared stimulated luminescence (IRSL) already exist. This landscape on the western margin of the Great Basin preserves exceptionally well-developed Pleistocene moraines and outwash deposits, providing an ideal natural setting for evaluating which glacial depositional environments most effectively reset ESR signals, a requirement for ESR to yield accurate depositional ages. To achieve this, samples will be collected from landforms with known 10Be and IRSL ages, ensuring that our ESR-derived ages can be directly validated against independent chronologies. ESR measurements will be performed on quartz defect centers (Al, Ti-Li, Ti-H, or Ge) and the centers that reliably record depositional ages will be identified and catalogued systematically. This project represents the first systematic evaluation of ESR dating in glacial sediments in North America and will develop the first framework for assessing ESR viability across different glacial transport environments. By integrating ESR ages with existing chronologies, the study will refine the glacial history of the western Great Basin and establish a foundation for applying ESR to much older glacial deposits that currently lie beyond the reach of luminescence and cosmogenic nuclide techniques.
